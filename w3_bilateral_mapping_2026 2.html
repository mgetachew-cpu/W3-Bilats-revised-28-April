What we are doing and why
By end of June 2026, deliver an updated mapping of W3/bilateral projects to Programmes and Accelerators covering at least 90% of each centre's portfolio by dollar value. This is a formally approved OKR (KR1.7). Two parts: (1) reviewing and signalling on existing 2025 mapped projects, and (2) centres mapping new projects from the updated Anaplan extract.

The completed mapping directly informs two confirmed downstream uses: centre delegate assignments in July and 2026 technical reporting. Other potential uses — projected benefits, 2027 PORBs, critical capabilities — are pending further decisions.
The 90% target is a formally committed Key Result under CGIAR's OKR framework:
KR1.7 — 90% of Centers' W3 and bilaterally funded work (by value) included in 2026 technical reports, by Q2 2027.
This sets the ambition: CGIAR's technical reports should reflect the near-totality of what Centers are doing across all funding sources, not just W1/W2. The 2025–30 Portfolio explicitly aims to encompass all of CGIAR's work across all centres and all sources of funding.

In 2025, the 80% threshold was met (285 projects, ~25% of active projects by number). Going to at least 90% is likely to encompass 500–600 projects.

GST confirmed (28 April): centres can map any active project within the 90% threshold — not just largest-to-smallest. Mapping above 90% is also permitted, though the delegate assignment threshold stays at 90%.
Mapping is one step in a broader process of portfolio alignment and cohesion-building — not a tick-box exercise. Three layers:
Administrative mapping — the mechanisms through which centres and P/As agree on which W3/bilat project fits where, and knock-on effects (delegate assignment, critical capabilities).
Operational cohesion — sharing and using planned and achieved delivery intentionally across funding streams, not after the fact.
Design alignment — common interoperability building blocks (indicators, impact pathways) baked into project design from the start, reducing costs of retrofitting and double reporting. A key objective of the MELIAF project.
The GST has also confirmed that mapping should be used to identify synergies, new funding opportunities, and to inform P/A prioritisation and internal W1/W2 allocation — while keeping additional reporting at a minimum viable level.
Two uses are confirmed:
Centre delegate assignments — July 2026, directly driven by completed mapping (threshold at 90%).
2026 technical reporting — at least 90% of W3/bilat work by value to be included in P/A reports by Q2 2027 (KR1.7). AWG to refine reporting modalities by 30 June based on 2025 experience.
The following are proposed but not yet fully resolved:
Projected benefits (2026) — GST discussed and wants the exercise to continue with W3/bilat included. However, results will be assessed before any allocation decisions are made — not automatic. How W3/bilat is incorporated more explicitly is still being worked out.
2027 PORBs — whether mapped project KPIs will be integrated into P/A Theories of Change and plans of results is not yet confirmed.
Critical capabilities (2026) — potential linkage to mapping outcomes, direction not yet confirmed.
The core process — at least 90% mapping target, centre delegate assignment, technical reporting — is agreed and underway. The GST has now also confirmed that centres can map any active projects within the threshold (not just largest-first) and that mapping can exceed 90%. Remaining open questions (KPI linkage to PORBs, how W3/bilat feeds into projected benefits allocations, stage gating implications) are expected to be resolved over Q2–Q3 2026.
Using the signal module (closes 1 May)
A temporary tool open until 1 May 2026 for capturing P/A signals on existing mapped projects. Log in at: cgiar-program-center-project-mapping.fly.dev using your programme-specific access code and password provided by Elvin. SSO is not available in this tool.

The full mapping module is a separate, permanent tool at project-mapping.cgiar.org — going live on 18 May for centres. Contact Elvin for login issues; use the in-tool feedback form for other feedback. Questions: performanceandresults@cgiar.org. Resources also on the P&R Hub W3/Bilat page.
For each project: title (with description on hover), timeline, value, and a reporting indicator showing whether it reported results in the 2025 cycle. Full result detail is available via the Results Dashboard linked in the tool, or the CGIAR CG Results Dashboard. The original mapping rationale (complementarity and strategic benefits) is visible when you click into a project.

Fields sourced from Anaplan (dates, budget, etc.) are read-only — if any data looks wrong, it must be corrected in Anaplan first.
Keep as is is the default — if you are happy with the current mapping, no action needed on that project. For changes:

• Adjust percentage — use the slider or type a value. Add a message and save.
• Remove — click remove (sets percentage to 0), add context, save.
• Increase — also possible via the slider.

An Excel export of your full project list with all signals and timestamps is available for records. If you prefer to work offline, download the Excel, share with colleagues, then re-enter signals in the tool.
Two dimensions, each rated high / medium / low:
Complementarity of results — does the W3/bilat clearly contribute to the objectives and Theory of Change of your P/A? Does its topic fall within your remit?
Efficiencies / strategic benefit — is there geographic focus overlap or strategic expansion potential? Is there partnership overlap or strategic expansion?
These are the same criteria used in the original 2025 mapping and are embedded in the tool's comment function. Use them as the basis for any message you attach to a signal.

Process and decisions
Centre-led. P/As provide a signal — it is input for centres to consider, not a binding decision. In phase 2 (from 18 May), centres map projects and P/As accept or reject those proposals via the full mapping module. Disagreements are resolved via the built-in chat; historically most cases have been resolved bilaterally without GST escalation.
Yes. The signal module allows P/As to signal an increase in the mapped percentage for a project. Use the slider and attach a message explaining the rationale using the complementarity and strategic benefit criteria. The centre will receive this signal and decide whether to act on it.
Yes — the GST endorsed "at least 90%", so mapping above 90% is permitted and encouraged for portfolio cohesion and technical reporting purposes. However, the threshold for calculating centre delegate assignments stays at 90%. Anything mapped above 90% does not further change delegate allocation but does count for reporting and cohesion objectives.

Centres should be mindful of transaction costs — mapping more projects means more to report on effectively.
Timing is not ideal this year — ADAPT PORBs are being submitted now, but updated mapping is not complete until June. No further PORB action needed right now. The PPT will append the updated W3/bilateral project list to your ADAPT PORB once mapping is finalised.

Whether mapped W3/bilat project KPIs will feed into 2027 PORBs is a separate question not yet confirmed by the GST.
Centre delegates to P/As are assigned in July based directly on the completed mapping — which centres have projects mapped to which P/As (up to the 90% threshold) determines delegate positions. This is the most proximate confirmed dependency and the main reason the June deadline matters.

Whether delegate assignments will be reviewed annually is not yet confirmed — the Delegate Terms of Reference do not specify a time limit. The PPT will share the centre delegate allocation formula so centres can see exactly how mapping informs assignment.
